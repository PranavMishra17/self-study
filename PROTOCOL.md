# Protocol

The curriculum is the easy part. This file is the part that decides whether any of
it survives contact with a normal month.

---

## Week states

Every week gets one, declared at the start when you can see what the week looks
like, and corrected at the end if reality disagreed.

**Green — the full five hours.** Sessions A, B and C all happen.

**Amber — two to three hours.** Sessions A and B only. Session C is dropped without
guilt or replacement. This is the normal state of a busy week and it is not failure.

**Red — sixty to ninety minutes.** Session B only, the mathematics, because it is
the track that decays fastest and is cheapest to keep alive. Nothing else. A Red
week counts as an unbroken week. This matters more than it sounds: the streak is
what you are protecting, not the hours.

**Interview sprint.** A live loop means Red weeks for as long as it takes. Not a
suspension of the plan, a state of it. Declare it, sprint, come back on the ramp.
This is the exact situation that ended the last attempt, and the only difference
this time is that a Red week has a definition and a way out.

## Re-entry ramp

After two or more consecutive Red weeks, the next non-Red week **repeats the last
session A you actually completed** rather than advancing to a new one. It will feel
like a waste of a week. It is not. Re-entering at the wrong depth is why people
bounce off a plan permanently rather than temporarily.

After four or more consecutive Red weeks, re-take the last gate quiz before
advancing. If it fails, the milestone extends. No shame attaches to this.

## No back-filling

Missed hours are never owed. There is no catching up, no double session, no debt.
A missed week is simply gone. Accumulated debt is the most common reason long plans
get dropped, so this one does not create any.

## Three working rules

Not character corrections. Just mechanics that make hard material easier to stay
with, and that stop anything difficult from quietly disappearing.

**The ten-minute floor.** Before marking something unknown or moving past it, give
it ten minutes of written attempt — what you know, where it stops, what exactly is
missing. Most things resolve inside that window. The ones that do not are now
precisely described, which is far more useful later than a blank.

**The requeue.** Anything you set aside goes into `REQUEUE.md` and comes back
later. Stopping defers; it does not delete. This is what makes stopping fine — you
are allowed to move on, the item just does not vanish.

**The weekly look.** Once a week, open the requeue and read it. Not to clear it,
just to keep it in view.

## The wildcard

A sixth slot sits on the planner, normally empty. When an interview loop is
scheduled, sessions for it are written into that slot rather than into the weeks,
with the same structure as anything else: steps, pointers, a closing quiz.

Closing a wildcard session earns one credit, and a credit folds one planned session
in — counted as covered without being done separately, because interview prep
genuinely does cover some of this material. **The cap is four folds out of fifteen
sessions**, and that cap is the entire point: interview work can cover part of the
plan, never most of it. Folds are reversible, and the folded session keeps its
material.

Full detail in `WILDCARD.md`.

## The re-plan ritual

At the end of each milestone, before starting the next:

1. Take the gate quiz. Record the result in `diagnostics/`.
2. Read `CHARTER.md`. All of it, not from memory.
3. Ask the three questions: what did I actually do, what did I avoid, and what did I
   learn about how I work.
4. Detail the next milestone. Promote the sketch. Add a new sketch at the back.
5. Write the deviations into `CHANGELOG.md` — specifically, anything that now
   contradicts the charter, with the reason.
6. Clear the wildcard slot. Anything folded but not genuinely covered goes into
   `REQUEUE.md` rather than disappearing.

Step five is the anti-drift mechanism. Without it, five reasonable local decisions
become a plan nobody chose.

## Session hygiene

Session A has one rule and it is absolute: **nothing else is open.** Not Claude
Code, not the applications tab, not the phone. Interleaving works for applications
because applications are shallow and batchable. It does not work for this. A session
A with three other things running did not happen, regardless of the hours logged.

Sessions B and C are more forgiving. Split them, move them, do B in two sittings.

Mornings at 07:00, before the first meeting, is the stated intention. It is a good
one — the two hours before anything starts are the only reliably uncontested hours
in the day.
